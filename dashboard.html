Four business questions

The answers come straight from the forecast above. Each one ends with what I would do about it.

1. Which products will bring in the most revenue next year?

Retro Sneakers, Vintage Denim Jacket and Used Smartphone make about €34k each. The whole store is forecast at about €242k, up from €232k in 2025. Put the most sourcing effort and shelf space into these three.

2. Which products are growing and which are shrinking?

Paperback Books Box (+18%), Oak Bookshelf (+11%) and Ceramic Dinner Set (+6%) grow the most. The Mid-century Armchair is the only clear decline (−10%). Source more books and bookshelves, and review the armchair’s price and sourcing.

3. When should we stock up?

Store-wide sales peak in October and November and are quietest in March, but each product has its own season. Buy winter coats from September and sneakers from March.

Darker means busier than that product’s own average. The amber outline marks its peak month.

4. Which forecasts can we trust?

Clothing is the most reliable (error 13–17%). Mid-century Armchair and Vintage Camera are above 40% because they sell only 10–15 a month. In the backtest the model was too low for 7 of 12 products, so plan stock from the clothing forecasts and order a bit more than the model says.

Bias: negative means the model predicted less than what really sold.

Forecast error: amber means above 35%, so treat it as a rough range.

Data is synthetic, generated in Python and stored in PostgreSQL. Model: gradient boosting on lagged sales and seasonality, one model per product.
